# Module 19 — Buying a Boat in Nova Scotia: A Practical Guide

## 19.1 First: get the sequence right / Спочатку: правильна послідовність

**🇨🇦 EN** — You're planning to buy for next season. Good — that's exactly the right amount of lead time, and it lets you do this in the right order. The single most common expensive mistake is buying before you know what you want.

**The recommended sequence, and why:**

| When | Do this | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug–Oct 2026** | Sail as much as possible. Crew on other people's boats. Go aboard everything you can. | You are gathering the only data that matters: what you actually like. Ask every owner "what would you buy next time, and why?" |
| **Oct–Nov 2026** | Volunteer for haul-out days. | **The single best free education in what breaks.** You'll see hulls out of the water, corroded seacocks, tired rigging, and worn cutless bearings — with an owner explaining as they go. After two haul-outs you can spot a neglected boat from the dock. |
| **Winter 2026/27** | Research and shortlist. ROC(M) and Coastal Navigation courses. Read *This Old Boat*. Watch listings **without buying** — you're learning the market's price/condition curve. | Winter is when you become an informed buyer instead of a hopeful one. |
| **May–Jun 2027** | **Do Basic Cruising first.** | This matters practically, not just as advice: insurers ask about your qualifications, and it's much cheaper to discover you dislike sailing 9 m boats on someone else's than on your own. |
| **Aug–Oct 2027** | **Buy in the autumn.** | This is the real tip in this module. Autumn is a **buyer's market** — sellers who didn't sell over summer are facing another winter's storage bill and are far more flexible. You also get the whole winter to fix, learn, and prepare the boat instead of losing sailing weeks to it. |
| **May 2028** | Launch your own boat, ready. | You start the season sailing, not sanding. |

**🇨🇦 EN — If you'd rather buy in spring 2027**, that's a perfectly reasonable choice — you get a full season aboard immediately. Just know the trade: spring is a seller's market with less inventory and firmer prices, and anything the survey turns up has to be fixed *now*, in the middle of the launch rush when every yard and tradesperson is booked solid.

**🇺🇦 UA** — Ви плануєте купити на наступний сезон. Добре — це саме правильний запас часу, і він дозволяє зробити все в правильному порядку. Найпоширеніша дорога помилка — купити, перш ніж ви знаєте, чого хочете.

**Рекомендована послідовність:** серпень–жовтень 2026 — ходіть скільки можете на чужих судах → жовтень–листопад — волонтерте на підйомі суден на берег (**найкраща безкоштовна освіта щодо того, що ламається**) → зима 2026/27 — дослідження, короткий список, курси ROC(M) і навігації, спостереження за оголошеннями **без покупки** → травень–червень 2027 — **спочатку зробіть Basic Cruising** (страховики питають про кваліфікації, і дешевше з'ясувати свої смаки на чужому судні) → **серпень–жовтень 2027 — купуйте восени**.

**🇺🇦 UA — Головна порада цього модуля: купуйте восени.** Осінь — **ринок покупця**: продавці, які не продали за літо, стоять перед ще одним рахунком за зимове зберігання і значно гнучкіші. Плюс ви отримуєте всю зиму на ремонт і підготовку замість того, щоб втрачати тижні сезону.

## 19.2 What boat, for this coast / Яке судно для цього берега

**🇨🇦 EN** — Nova Scotia is cold, foggy, rocky, has genuine Atlantic swell and a hurricane season. That narrows the field usefully. And since you'll be sailing with your partner and daughter, **ease of handling matters as much as capability** — see [Module 20](20-family-sailing.md).

### The size question

| Length | Rough price band (used, sound) | Realistic for you? |
|---|---|---|
| **20–24 ft** | $3,000–15,000 | Cheap, simple, trailerable. But cramped for three, minimal shelter, and rough in Atlantic swell. A fine *first* boat if you want to learn cheaply and sell on. |
| **26–30 ft** | $12,000–45,000 | **The sweet spot for a Halifax family cruiser.** Standing headroom (in most), a real cabin, a head, an enclosed berth for a child, capable of the South Shore, and still handleable by two people. Haul-out and storage costs stay sane. |
| **31–36 ft** | $35,000–110,000 | More comfort, more speed, more genuine offshore capability — and meaningfully more money in every direction: bigger sails, bigger winches, higher storage and yard fees, more systems to fail. |
| **37 ft+** | $90,000+ | Not a first boat. Running costs escalate sharply. |

**Recommendation: look hard at 27–32 ft.** With three of you and a daughter who'll grow, that's the band where the boat is genuinely comfortable for a South Shore cruise without becoming a second job.

### Canadian and East Coast boats worth shortlisting

**🇨🇦 EN** — These come up regularly in Nova Scotia, were often built in Canada, and suit this coast. This is a starting list for research, not a recommendation of any specific hull — condition matters far more than model.

| Boat | Roughly | Why it's on the list |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonsuch 26 / 30 / 33** | $25,000–70,000 | **Look at these seriously for a family.** Canadian-built (Hinterhoeller, Ontario). A single unstayed mast with one wishbone-boomed sail: **no jib to change, no foredeck work, and nothing to send you forward in a blow.** Enormous interior for the length, very easy for two people to handle. The trade: unconventional, less upwind performance, and a distinctive look you'll either love or not. |
| **Alberg 30** | $12,000–30,000 | An East Coast classic — narrow, heavy, full keel, famously seakindly in a swell. Slow, small interior, very seaworthy. A cult following for good reason. |
| **Contessa 26** | $10,000–25,000 | Canadian-built, tiny, and genuinely offshore-capable — this design has crossed oceans. Very small for three people, but superb value and confidence-inspiring. |
| **CS 27 / 30 / 33 / 36** | $18,000–65,000 | Canadian Sailcraft. Well-built, well-regarded, good balance of performance and cruising comfort. A 1986 CS 30 was recently listed around **$27,500 in Chester**. |
| **Grampian 26 / 30** | $8,000–25,000 | Canadian-built, plentiful, inexpensive, and all over the Nova Scotia coast. Basic and dated, but honest boats and cheap to get into. |
| **Tanzer 22 / 26 / 7.5** | $5,000–20,000 | Quebec-built, very common, simple and affordable. Good learner boats. |
| **C&C 27 / 30 / 34** | $15,000–55,000 | Canadian, more performance-oriented. Fast and fun; generally less interior volume than a comparable cruiser. |
| **Catalina 27 / 30** | $12,000–40,000 | American, produced in huge numbers, so parts and knowledge are everywhere. Roomy interiors. Less heavily built than an Alberg — fine for coastal work. |
| **Cape Dory 28 / 30** | $20,000–45,000 | Traditional, heavily built, full keel, very pretty. Seaworthy and slow. |
| **Bayfield 25 / 29 / 32** | $15,000–45,000 | Canadian-built (Ontario), traditional cutter styling, shallow draft — which is genuinely useful on this coast. |

### Features that actually matter here

- ☐ **Inboard diesel, and evidence it's been maintained.** Fog, calms, and lee shores make the engine safety equipment. A tired outboard on a 30-footer is a real limitation. **Ask for engine hours and service records; a well-documented old engine beats an undocumented newer one.**
- ☐ **Draft around 1.2–1.6 m.** Deeper closes off South Shore coves.
- ☐ **A dodger, or the fittings for one.** On a cold, wet, foggy coast this is the difference between your family enjoying it and enduring it. Budget $1,500–3,500 for a new one.
- ☐ **Cabin heating**, or space to fit it. Extends your season at both ends. A diesel or propane heater runs $1,500–4,000 installed.
- ☐ **Wheel or tiller?** Tiller is simpler, cheaper, more feel, and gives more cockpit space when lashed. Wheel is easier for guests and long stints. Either is fine.
- ☐ **A cockpit three people can actually sit in**, and a companionway a child can go up and down safely.
- ☐ **Oversized ground tackle.** Rocky, weedy bottoms. Go one anchor size up.
- ☐ **A proper boarding ladder** that works from in the water, not just from a dock.
- ☐ **Simple systems.** Every clever system is a future failure. Fewer, better.

**🇺🇦 UA — Розмір:** дивіться уважно на **27–32 фути**. З трьома людьми і дочкою, яка росте, це діапазон, де судно справді комфортне для круїзу по Південному узбережжю, не перетворюючись на другу роботу.

**🇺🇦 UA — Канадські судна, варті короткого списку:** **Nonsuch 26/30/33** (розгляньте серйозно для родини — одна незакріплена мачта з одним вітрилом: **немає стакселя, немає роботи на баку, нічого не відправляє вас уперед при вітрі**; величезний інтер'єр, дуже легко керувати вдвох) · **Alberg 30** (класика Східного узбережжя, важкий, повний кіль, дуже м'який на хвилі) · **Contessa 26** (канадський, крихітний, справді океанський) · **CS 27/30/33** (Canadian Sailcraft, добре побудовані; CS 30 1986 року нещодавно виставляли близько **$27 500 у Chester**) · **Grampian 26/30** (канадські, численні, недорогі, по всьому берегу НШ) · **Tanzer** (Квебек, прості й доступні) · **C&C** (канадські, швидкіші) · **Catalina 27/30** (величезні тиражі, тому запчастини й знання всюди) · **Bayfield** (мала осадка — справді корисно тут).

**🇺🇦 UA — Що справді важливо тут:** стаціонарний дизель з історією обслуговування (**запитуйте моточаси й сервісні записи**) · осадка 1,2–1,6 м · **дожер** (на холодному мокрому туманному берегі це різниця між задоволенням і терпінням родини; новий $1 500–3 500) · обігрів каюти ($1 500–4 000 з установкою) · кокпіт, у якому справді сидять троє · збільшений якір · нормальний трап з води · прості системи.

## 19.3 Where to look / Де шукати

| Source | What it's good for |
|---|---|
| **Yacht club noticeboards and word of mouth** | **Start here.** Frequently the best boats at the best prices, from members whose maintenance history is *knowable*. In a community the size of Halifax's, you can ask around about a specific boat and learn things no listing will tell you. This is a genuine advantage of joining a club before you buy. |
| **Kijiji** (Halifax / Nova Scotia sailboat sections) | The largest pool of private local listings. Wide range of quality — lots of projects and abandoned dreams alongside good boats. |
| **BoatDealers.ca** | Aggregates Canadian dealer and private listings. |
| **YachtWorld** (Nova Scotia filter) | Broker listings, generally higher-end and higher-priced, but well documented. |
| **Ocean Yacht Sales** | Brokerage covering Canada's Atlantic provinces. |
| **Atlantic Yacht Sales** | Nova Scotia brokerage. |
| **Facebook groups** for Nova Scotia / Maritime sailing | Where boats get mentioned before they're formally listed. |
| **Walk the yards in late October** | Boats coming out with "for sale" signs, owners who've just decided, and you can see the whole hull. Genuinely the best time and place to shop. |

**🇨🇦 EN — Broker or private sale?** A broker costs the seller a commission (so it's arguably in the price), but handles the paperwork, title search, and lien check — which is real value if you're buying your first boat. A private sale can be cheaper and more transparent about history. Either way, **you** commission the survey.

**🇺🇦 UA — Де шукати:** **починайте з дошок об'яв у яхт-клубах і сарафанного радіо** (часто найкращі судна за найкращими цінами, від людей, чию історію обслуговування можна дізнатися) · Kijiji (найбільший пул приватних оголошень, дуже різна якість) · BoatDealers.ca · YachtWorld з фільтром Нової Шотландії · Ocean Yacht Sales · Atlantic Yacht Sales · групи у Facebook · **пройдіться майданчиками в кінці жовтня** (судна виходять з води з табличками «продається», і ви бачите весь корпус — справді найкращий час і місце).

## 19.4 The survey — never skip it / Сюрвей — ніколи не пропускайте

**🇨🇦 EN** — A marine surveyor is the best money you will spend in this whole process.

| Item | Typical cost |
|---|---|
| **Pre-purchase survey**, 26–32 ft | Commonly quoted at **$15–30 per foot** → roughly **$450–900** for a 30-footer. Some surveyors bill differently: e.g. **$20/ft LOA + a travel fee + HST**, or a base rate plus a per-foot surcharge that increases for boats over 20 and over 30 years old. |
| **Haul-out for the survey** | **$200–600**, depending on the yard. A survey without hauling the boat is not a full survey. |
| **Engine survey / oil analysis** (separate specialist) | **$300–700.** Worth it on any boat where the engine is a significant part of the value. |
| **Rig inspection** (may be extra, or require unstepping the mast) | **$200–600+** |

**🇨🇦 EN — Note on insurance:** as of mid‑2024 at least one major Canadian marine insurer (Gallagher Skippers' Plan) waives the survey requirement for boats **under 30 ft with a market value under $50,000**. **Do not let that talk you out of a survey.** The insurer is managing *their* risk on a low-value hull; you are trying to avoid buying a $30,000 problem. Get the survey anyway.

### What a surveyor is looking for, and what should scare you

| Finding | How bad |
|---|---|
| **Wet deck core / soft spots** underfoot or around fittings | 🔴 **Serious.** Water in a balsa or plywood deck core. Proper repair is thousands and it's messy. Very common on older boats. |
| **Structural hull blisters / advanced osmosis** | 🔴 **Serious** if extensive. A peel-and-dry job is a five-figure sum. Scattered cosmetic blisters are usually tolerable. |
| **Corroded or cracked chainplates**, or rust weeping from the deck around them | 🔴 **Serious.** These hold the mast up. |
| **Rigging over 15–20 years old** | 🟠 Budget to replace it. **Standing rigging replacement on a 30-footer: $3,000–8,000.** Insurers increasingly ask about rig age. |
| **Keel-hull joint cracks, or evidence of a grounding** | 🔴 Investigate hard. Keel bolt problems are expensive and serious. |
| **Engine: hard starting, blue/white smoke, low compression, no service records** | 🟠 A repower is **$8,000–20,000+** installed. This single item can invert the economics of a cheap boat. |
| **Seized or corroded seacocks** | 🟠 Each is a sinking risk. Replacement **$150–400 each**, and a boat has many. |
| **Soft or delaminated bulkheads, rot in a wooden mast step** | 🔴 Structural. |
| **Tired sails** (baggy, blown out, UV-damaged) | 🟠 A new main and headsail for a 30-footer: **$4,000–9,000**. Serviceable used sails, far less. |
| **Old wiring, no breaker panel, "creative" electrical work** | 🟠 Fire risk. Rewiring is slow, expensive labour. |
| **Deferred cosmetics** — faded gelcoat, tired brightwork, ugly cushions | 🟢 **Ignore this, and use it to negotiate.** Cosmetics are the cheapest thing on a boat and they scare other buyers off. **An ugly boat with a good hull, rig and engine is the bargain you want.** |

**🇨🇦 EN — The rule:** buy on **hull, rig, engine and keel**. Everything else is money and weekends. A pretty boat with a wet deck and a dying engine is a trap; a scruffy boat that's structurally sound is an opportunity.

**🇺🇦 UA — Сюрвей:** **передпродажний сюрвей 26–32 фути** зазвичай **$15–30 за фут** → приблизно **$450–900** для 30-футового. Деякі сюрвейєри рахують як **$20/фут + виїзд + HST**, або базова ставка плюс надбавка за вік (більше для суден понад 20 і понад 30 років). **Підйом на берег для сюрвею $200–600** — сюрвей без підйому це не повний сюрвей. **Сюрвей двигуна $300–700.**

**🇺🇦 UA — Про страхування:** станом на середину 2024 року щонайменше один великий канадський морський страховик знімає вимогу сюрвею для суден **менше 30 футів вартістю менше $50 000**. **Не дозволяйте цьому відмовити вас від сюрвею.** Страховик керує *своїм* риском; ви намагаєтесь не купити проблему за $30 000.

**🇺🇦 UA — Правило:** купуйте за **корпусом, такелажем, двигуном і кілем**. Усе інше — це гроші й вихідні. Красиве судно з мокрою палубою і вмираючим двигуном — це пастка; облізле, але конструктивно здорове — це можливість. **Занедбана косметика — це те, чим ви торгуєтесь**: вона відштовхує інших покупців.

## 19.5 The real cost of ownership / Справжня вартість володіння

**🇨🇦 EN** — Here is a worked annual budget for a **30-foot boat bought at around $30,000** in the Halifax area. This is the number people don't calculate before buying, and it is why boats get abandoned in yards.

| Annual item | Estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Club membership** | $600–1,800 | Full membership at a Halifax club. Varies a lot — ask each club for its current dues schedule. |
| **Mooring or slip** | $1,200–3,500 | A mooring ball with tender service is cheaper than an alongside slip. Dartmouth YC, for example, offers both berths (up to 44 ft) and mooring balls. Rates are usually per foot. |
| **Winter storage ashore** | $700–2,000 | Typically per foot. In-water winter storage in a bubbled marina is an alternative some clubs offer. |
| **Haul-out and launch** | $500–1,200 | Yard crane time plus labour. AYC publishes yard labour at around **$62/hour** (half-hour minimum) if their crew does the work — doing more yourself saves real money. |
| **Insurance** | $300–650 | Sailboats commonly run **0.75–1.5% of insured value** per year — on a $30,000 boat that's roughly $225–450, though minimum premiums and your (lack of) experience push it up. **Your Sail Canada certifications directly help here.** |
| **Antifouling paint + anodes + consumables** | $350–800 | A gallon of decent antifoul is $150–350; you'll want anodes, sandpaper, rollers, and rags every spring. |
| **Engine service** | $200–600 | Oil, filters, impeller, belts. Cheap if you do it yourself — and [Module 13](13-engine-and-docking.md) says you should learn to. |
| **PCL (boat licence)** | **Free** | Required for engines ≥10 hp / 7.5 kW. |
| **Maintenance / repair fund** | **$1,500–3,000** | 🔴 **Do not omit this line.** Something fails every year. Sails, rigging, electronics, upholstery, a pump, the head. Budgeting zero here is the mistake. |
| **Fuel** | $150–400 | Sailboats use remarkably little. |
| **Safety gear renewal** | $100–300 | Flares expire every four years; inflatable PFDs need servicing. |
| **TOTAL, annual** | **≈ $5,600–14,850** | Commonly lands around **10–20% of the boat's value per year** for an older boat. |

### And the one-time costs at purchase

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | $30,000 |
| Survey + haul for survey + engine survey | $1,000–2,000 |
| Tax on the sale | Verify current provincial rules for a private vs brokered boat sale |
| Immediate must-fix items from the survey | **$2,000–8,000** — assume there will be some |
| Safety equipment to full legal standard, plus a grab bag | $600–1,500 |
| Ground tackle upgrade | $500–1,500 |
| Dodger, if not fitted | $1,500–3,500 |
| Personal gear for three people | $1,200–2,500 |
| **Realistic all-in first-year cost** | **≈ $40,000–50,000** on a $30,000 boat |

**🇨🇦 EN — Read that last line twice.** A $30,000 boat costs roughly $40,000–50,000 to get on the water and keep there for a year. That's not a reason not to buy — it's the number to plan against so the boat stays a pleasure rather than a source of financial resentment.

**🇺🇦 UA — Справжня вартість володіння,** робочий річний бюджет для **30-футового судна за ~$30 000** у районі Галіфаксу: членство в клубі $600–1 800 · бочка або місце в марині $1 200–3 500 · зимове зберігання на берегі $700–2 000 · підйом і спуск $500–1 200 (AYC публікує тариф робіт близько **$62/год**, тож робити більше самому — реальна економія) · страхування $300–650 (**0,75–1,5% від застрахованої вартості** на рік; **ваші сертифікати Sail Canada тут прямо допомагають**) · фарба проти обростання, аноди, розхідники $350–800 · обслуговування двигуна $200–600 · PCL **безкоштовно** · 🔴 **фонд ремонту $1 500–3 000 — не пропускайте цей рядок** · паливо $150–400 · оновлення спорядження безпеки $100–300.

**🇺🇦 UA — Разом: ≈ $5 600–14 850 на рік**, зазвичай **10–20% вартості судна на рік** для старшого судна. А **разові витрати при покупці** доводять реалістичну суму першого року до **≈ $40 000–50 000 на судні за $30 000**. Це не причина не купувати — це число, під яке треба планувати, щоб судно залишалося задоволенням, а не джерелом фінансової образи.

## 19.6 Consider not buying / Розгляньте варіант не купувати

**🇨🇦 EN** — Genuinely weigh these before committing, because for a family in your position one of them may be better:

| Option | Roughly per year | The honest case for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Club membership + crewing** | $200–600 | Free sailing on other people's boats. Zero maintenance. You'll sail more varied boats than you ever would as an owner. **Never stop doing this even if you buy.** |
| **Sailing co-op / fractional share** | $1,500–4,000 | **The strongest alternative for a family.** You get scheduled, regular access and real time in command, someone else handles a share of the maintenance, and the cost is a fraction of ownership. If a co-op exists in Halifax for a boat you like, this may beat buying outright. |
| **Local charter, day or week** | $300–900/day | Perfect for "let's see if the family actually enjoys a weekend aboard" — before you find out on a boat you own. Requires certification. |
| **Buy a cheap small boat first** ($5,000–12,000, 22–26 ft) | $2,500–5,000 | Learn ownership on a boat where mistakes are cheap, then trade up in two years knowing exactly what you want. A genuinely smart path, and the one many experienced owners say they wish they'd taken. |
| **Own a dinghy, charter a cruiser** | $1,000–3,000 | A small dinghy for you and your daughter to sail together weekly (see [Module 20](20-family-sailing.md)), plus a chartered cruiser for the two or three real trips a year. Often the best value of all for a family. |

**🇨🇦 EN — The question to answer honestly:** *what do I want to do that these options don't allow?* If you have a clear answer — a specific boat, a specific cruise, the pleasure of tinkering, or wanting the boat available on a whim with your family's stuff already aboard — buy. Those are all good reasons. If you don't have a clear answer yet, another season of crewing costs almost nothing and will produce one.

**🇺🇦 UA — Розгляньте варіант не купувати:** членство в клубі + робота в екіпажі ($200–600/рік, безкоштовне плавання на чужих судах, нуль обслуговування) · **вітрильний кооператив або часткова участь ($1 500–4 000 — найсильніша альтернатива для родини:** регулярний запланований доступ, реальний час у командуванні, частина обслуговування на комусь іншому) · локальний чартер ($300–900/день — ідеально, щоб з'ясувати, чи справді родині подобаються вихідні на борту) · **спочатку купити дешеве мале судно** ($5 000–12 000, 22–26 футів — вчитися володінню там, де помилки дешеві; шлях, про який багато досвідчених власників кажуть, що жалкують, що не пішли ним) · **власний дінгі + чартер круїзера** (часто найкраща вартість для родини).

**🇺🇦 UA — Питання, на яке треба чесно відповісти:** *що я хочу робити, чого ці варіанти не дозволяють?* Якщо є чітка відповідь — купуйте. Якщо чіткої відповіді ще немає, ще один сезон у екіпажі коштує майже нічого і цю відповідь дасть.

## 19.7 Purchase checklist / Контрольний список покупки

**Before you look**
- ☐ Total budget decided, **including** survey, immediate repairs, and the first year's running costs
- ☐ Basic Cruising certification in hand (or booked)
- ☐ Berth or mooring availability confirmed **before** you buy — a boat with nowhere to go is a problem
- ☐ Winter storage confirmed
- ☐ Insurance quoted for the type and size you're considering
- ☐ Shortlist of 3–5 models, and a clear list of your must-haves

**Viewing**
- ☐ Ask for **engine hours and service records** — documentation is a proxy for how the whole boat was treated
- ☐ Ask **"why are you selling?"** and listen to the whole answer
- ☐ Ask **"what would you fix next?"**
- ☐ Check every locker, lift every floorboard, **smell the bilge** (diesel, sewage, and mildew all tell you something)
- ☐ Press the deck around every fitting and stanchion base — feel for softness
- ☐ Look for rust weeping from chainplates and around keel bolts
- ☐ Check sail condition in daylight, unrolled
- ☐ **Start the engine from cold** yourself, and watch the exhaust
- ☐ Count and test the seacocks
- ☐ Bring someone experienced. A second pair of eyes finds what you're too excited to see.

**Committing**
- ☐ Offer **subject to survey and sea trial**, in writing
- ☐ Commission **your own** surveyor — not one the seller or broker recommends
- ☐ Attend the survey. **Walk around with the surveyor and ask questions** — this is a paid education in your own boat.
- ☐ Sea trial: sail it, and run the engine hard for a sustained period at temperature
- ☐ **Renegotiate on the survey findings** — this is what the survey is for
- ☐ Verify clear title and **no liens**
- ☐ Confirm the tax position on the sale
- ☐ Insurance bound **before** you take delivery
- ☐ Transfer or apply for the **Pleasure Craft Licence**; display the number on the bow
- ☐ Get the seller to hand over the boat and **walk you through every system** — this is worth asking for explicitly and most sellers are glad to do it

**🇺🇦 UA — Контрольний список:** *до пошуку* — загальний бюджет включно з сюрвеєм і першим роком утримання · сертифікат Basic Cruising · **підтверджене місце стоянки й зимового зберігання до покупки** · страхова пропозиція · короткий список 3–5 моделей. *На огляді* — **моточаси і сервісні записи** · «чому продаєте?» і «що б ви ремонтували наступним?» · відкрийте кожен рундук, підніміть кожну дошку підлоги, **понюхайте трюм** · натисніть палубу навколо кожного кріплення · іржа з обушків вант і біля кільових болтів · вітрила при денному світлі · **запустіть двигун з холоду самі** · перевірте кінгстони · **візьміть досвідчену людину з собою**. *При угоді* — пропозиція **за умови сюрвею й ходових випробувань**, письмово · **свій власний** сюрвейєр, не рекомендований продавцем · **будьте присутні на сюрвеї й задавайте питання** · ходові випробування з навантаженням двигуна · **перегляд ціни за результатами сюрвею** · чистий титул, **відсутність застав** · страховка до приймання · передача PCL.
